## 1. Why M0

Chosen against the protocol's first criterion - a charter with real content at stake rather than the easiest available.

`M0` carries the corpus exemplar for Boundaries: a neighbour table discriminating the set from five adjacent sets by a single question, alongside an admission test and implicit exclusions.\
A careless conversion could flatten that table into a list, which made it the clearest available test of whether `E4` preserves argued content.\
It was also missing both of the hard concerns - Territory and Composition - so one trial exercised both.

`A0` was the alternative and is the better *second* trial: it already answers Composition, so it would have tested Territory alone, and its territory is the hardest in the corpus.

## 3. Triangulation against the population

The protocol's four questions, asked of both versions.

### 3.1 Does the charter describe the members that actually exist?

**Old:** No. It describes what methodology is in general and lists its members only through the generated index. A reader learns the boundary of the layer and nothing about what the eight procedures are *for* as a group.

**New:** Yes, and the territory table is the reason. Every one of the eight members is placed at a moment in a unit of work, and the placement was derived from each member's own `hydrate-when` trigger rather than imposed - `M6` and `M8` trigger on entering unfamiliar work, `M1` and `M2` on verifying, `M3` and `M5` on deciding what survives.

**Checked:** all eight members land in exactly one moment, and no moment is populated by a member whose trigger does not match it.

### 3.2 Would a reader route an excluded item correctly?

**Old:** Yes. The neighbour table is the strongest boundary statement in the corpus and the original already routed every excluded kind of rule to its proper layer.

**New:** Yes, identically - the table is untouched.

**Finding:** no improvement and no degradation. **That is the correct result.** The conversion's first duty was not to damage what already worked, and it did not.

### 3.3 Does the territory make a real gap findable, or merely sound complete?

This is the question the trial most needed to answer, because a territory that sounds complete and detects nothing is the named failure.

**Old:** no territory; no gap is findable by construction.

**New:** two gaps surfaced, and both are **specific to this population** rather than generic:

- *Committing to a design* holds one member, `M7`, and it is a heavyweight gate. Nothing covers a small design decision that does not warrant a full audit. That is visible only because the moments are laid out and one is visibly thin.
- *Handing over* - passing work in progress to another agent or a later session - has no procedure.

**The second gap was checked against the corpus, not asserted.**\
A search across all eight members found three apparent matches, all false: two used *resume* in an unrelated sense (resume a document test; a revived item does not resume), and one discussed handoff as an axiom concern - remove administrative friction - rather than as a procedure for doing it.\
The axioms `A4` and `A6` address handover as a property to preserve; no methodology member gives a procedure for performing it.\
The gap is real.

**Verdict on this question: improved, and materially.**\
The territory detects at least one real, previously unrecorded gap, and the gap is consequential: the corpus's reader starts cold by definition, which makes handover the moment its central constraint bites.

### 3.4 Does the composition describe how these members actually relate?

**Old:** silent. The citation graph between members existed and was stated nowhere.

**New:** derived from the measured citation graph, not reasoned:

| Claim in the new charter | Measured |
|---|---|
| `M1` and `M3` are primitives cited by three others | in-degree 3 each - the highest in the set |
| `M3`, `M4`, `M5` cite one another as a cluster | `M3->M4`, `M4->M3`, `M5->M3`, `M5->M4` |
| `M7` and `M8` are compound - cite others, cited by none | out-degree 3 and 1, in-degree 0 and 0 |

The composition section also carries the most useful single sentence the conversion produced: *adding a member should first ask whether it is a new primitive or a new composition of existing ones.*\
That is an admission heuristic derived from structure, and the original had no way to state it.

**Verdict on this question: improved.**

## 4. Against the two purposes

**The charter's own purpose** - help someone choose how to conduct work.\
The old charter answered *which layer does my rule belong in*.\
The new one additionally answers *which procedure applies at this point in my work*, by the territory table.\
That is the question a practitioner actually arrives with, and the old charter could not answer it except by reading all eight members.

**The intent behind a set** as [`E3`](../../entities/E3-set.md) defines it - that a population can be asked what it owes itself, including whether anything is missing.\
The old charter could not be asked that question.\
The new one answers it with two named, checked gaps.

---

## 5. Something the conversion found that no step asked for

**A tension inside the set.**\
The purpose says every methodology is declinable.\
`M7` describes itself as a required gate that every extensive design *must* pass - which is closer to a standing commitment than a situated move.\
Laying out composition made it visible, because `M7` was the one member whose relation to the others did not fit the "situated move" frame.\
The new charter records the tension without resolving it.\
**That is arguably the trial's most valuable output**: a real inconsistency in the set, invisible in the original, that a reader would previously have had to rediscover.

## 6. Reservations - and both bear on E4, not on M0

### 6.1 The territory is a judgement, and E4 cannot check that it is a good one

The five moments are defensible and were derived from the members.\
They are not the only possible partition - one could equally partition by *what is at risk* (correctness, durability, authority).\
`E4` requires a territory and can check that one is present.\
It cannot check that a territory is *well chosen*, and a poorly chosen one would pass while hiding gaps.

**Implication for E4:** state that a territory must be derived from the population and must be shown to detect at least one real gap or to justify that none exists - which is the test this trial applied by hand. A territory that cannot point at a gap or argue its absence is decoration.

### 6.2 The charter grew by 78 percent

73 lines to 130.\
Every added line answers a question the original could not, so none is padding by the protocol's own test.\
But thirteen charters growing by three quarters is a real cost in a corpus whose reader has limited context, and `A12` - precision context engineering - applies.

**Implication for E4:** nothing yet. One trial is not enough to say whether the growth is inherent to the standard or specific to a charter that was missing two whole concerns. `A0`, which already answers Composition, will be the comparison.

**The failure mode the protocol named did not occur.**\

**Recommendation.**\
Do not proceed straight to `M5.5`.\
Proceed to `M5.4` with one amendment supported by this trial - that a territory must show it detects a real gap or argue that none exists - and run a second trial on `A0` before converting the remaining eleven, to test whether the growth in section 6.2 generalises.

**Unrecorded findings this trial produced**, which belong in the backlog whatever is decided about the charter:

1. Methodology has no procedure for handing over work in progress.
2. Methodology has nothing proportionate for a small design decision.
3. `M7` may be an axiom-shaped rule in a methodology's place.
