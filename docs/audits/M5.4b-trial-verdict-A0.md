# M5.4b trial verdict - A0 axioms

Second trial conversion to [`E4`](../../entities/E4-charter.md), run after `E4` was amended to require that a territory earn itself and that growth be paid for.\
Its isolating purpose: determine whether the first trial's growth was inherent to the standard or specific to a charter missing two whole concerns.

```yaml
charter:      A0 axioms/README.md
baseline:     axioms/README.md at commit 629cf2c - git show 629cf2c:axioms/README.md (140 lines)
candidate:    M5.4b-trial-candidate-A0.md beside this file (191 lines) - NOT applied
population:   A1-A14, mandates, applicability tags and related edges read for all fourteen;
              corpus-wide citation counts measured for all fourteen
verdict:      IMPROVED, and growth paid for
```

---

## 1. Why A0, and what it isolates

`A0` was the first trial's recommended second case for two reasons.

It **already answers Composition** - orthogonal in statement, conjunctive in force, no apex member - so it tests Territory alone.\
If its growth matched `M0`'s, growth would be inherent to the standard.\
If it were smaller, `M0`'s growth would be explained by `M0` missing two concerns rather than one.

And it is the **hardest territory in the corpus.**\
A set of first principles invites a territory that sounds complete and detects nothing - "all the ways a system can fail" - which is precisely the failure `E4`'s new obligation exists to prevent.

## 5. Triangulation against the population

### 5.1 Does the charter describe the members that actually exist?

**Old:** partly. The applicability matrix describes when each axiom binds, accurately. Nothing describes what they protect.

**New:** yes. Every axiom is placed in exactly one row by what its mandate forbids.

**Checked, not asserted.**\
A partition can be plausible and arbitrary, so it was tested against the axioms' own declared relations.\
Of the thirty-five `related` edges axioms draw to one another, **fifteen fall within a row** - about double the seven an arbitrary five-way split would produce.\
The partition tracks structure the axioms already declare about themselves, which is `E4`'s *derived, not imposed* obligation met by measurement rather than by argument.

### 5.2 Would a reader route an excluded item correctly?

**Old and new identical.**\
The standing-commitment against situated-move boundary is untouched and was already strong.

### 5.3 Does the territory detect a real gap, or merely sound complete?

The question the trial most needed to answer, on the case most likely to fail it.

**Old:** no territory. `How the set composes` asserts orthogonality, which detects redundancy and cannot detect absence - exactly the limitation recorded in backlog `B3`.

**New:** one real gap, and one tested non-gap.

~~**No axiom protects against an adversary.**~~

**Corrected after director review.**\
The original framing was wrong, not merely overstated.\
Collaborating agents are assumed to act in good faith, so malice is out of scope by design.\
The territory that matters is accidental misalignment, and because this corpus serves a lone agent as much as a multi-agent system, the actor most likely to be wrong is the agent itself.

**The corrected gap: no axiom states the property that protects an agent against its own error.**\
The corpus defends against it in two places - standing doctrine in `AGENTS.md`, and the independence machinery of `M1` and `R3`.\
The doctrine is always in force and no axiom anchors it.\
The machinery needs a second actor, which a lone agent does not have.
**Checked:** nine of fourteen axioms bind without a second actor, and none states the property.

**Cost is not a gap.**\
The row titles suggest economy has no home.\
It does: `A11` and `A12` take economy as their mandate, and five further axioms cite it.\
Recording this matters as much as recording the real gap, because it shows the territory distinguishes an absence of answer from an absence of heading - the exact error the four-section checker made.

**Verdict on this question: improved, and this is the strongest result of either trial.**\
It is the case you named when proposing that a charter should expose coverage, weight and gaps, and it now does all three.

### 5.4 Does composition describe how these members actually relate?

**Old:** yes, already. Orthogonal and conjunctive; no apex.

**New:** unchanged, plus weight. The most cited axiom, `A8`, is referenced from 31 files; the least, `A9`, from 5.\
**The pointed finding:** `A9` is tagged `any-system`, so it binds every system unconditionally, and it is the least exercised axiom in the set.\
The charter states both readings rather than ruling - an axiom cited by nothing may be dead weight or may be holding a boundary nobody tests.

## 6. Against the two purposes

**The charter's own purpose** - decide whether a principle is a standing commitment, and which axioms bind the system in front of you.\
The old charter answered both.\
The new one adds a third question a reader actually brings: *is my system protected against this kind of failure?*\
That was previously answerable only by reading all fourteen mandates.

**The intent behind a set** - that a population can be asked what it owes itself, including whether anything is missing.\
The old charter could be asked only whether its members overlap.\
The new one can be asked what is missing, and answers with a checked gap.

**IMPROVED, and growth paid for.**

**Recommendation.**\
The two trials are sufficient to proceed to `M5.5`.\

Convert the remaining eleven one per change, each recorded with a short verdict in this form rather than the full treatment, since the method is now established.\
